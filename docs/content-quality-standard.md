# Decoded Justice — Content Quality Standard

## Purpose

Decoded Justice is a Washington-focused legal-information and case-organization platform. Content must help a user understand a question, locate the underlying authority, distinguish documented facts from interpretation, and identify the next research step without presenting legal information as a guaranteed outcome.

## Source hierarchy

When a proposition can be supported by primary authority, prefer the primary source:

1. Washington statutes (RCW), administrative rules (WAC), court rules, and official court materials.
2. Federal statutes, regulations, and official federal court materials when the issue is federal.
3. Official agency policies, procedures, forms, and guidance for agency-specific processes.
4. Official government or court explanatory materials.
5. Reputable secondary sources for explanation or orientation, clearly identified as secondary.

A secondary source must not be presented as though it is the controlling authority.

## Every substantive legal claim should be checked for

- Jurisdiction.
- Relevant court, agency, or governmental body.
- Effective date or other relevant time period.
- Whether the authority is current.
- Whether the proposition is a statute, rule, policy, case holding, factual statement, interpretation, or practical guidance.
- A source link or citation when the interface supports one.

## Case and evidence content

Case-management features must distinguish:

- FACT — supported by a record or direct user documentation.
- ALLEGATION — asserted by a person or source but not established by the available record.
- INFERENCE — a reasoned connection drawn from documented material.
- DISPUTED — materially contested by the parties or records.
- UNKNOWN — not established by the available information.

Do not convert an allegation into a fact merely because it appears repeatedly. Do not convert an inference into a legal conclusion without identifying the underlying authority and reasoning.

## Research behavior

Research tools should:

- Start with the user's actual question.
- Surface relevant source categories rather than imply a preferred legal outcome.
- Preserve source provenance where practical.
- Encourage verification against the linked authority.
- Record access dates for time-sensitive material.
- Make clear when a result is only a research lead.

## Washington specificity

Washington-specific content should identify Washington authority where applicable. Avoid silently substituting another state's law, a federal rule, or generalized legal information for Washington law.

## Dates and currency

Do not use phrases such as "current law" without a meaningful verification date when the content can change. When a guide depends on a deadline, effective date, filing period, or agency procedure, state the relevant date or instruct the user to verify the current rule.

## User-facing language

Use plain language first. Explain legal terminology when it is necessary. Avoid:

- guarantees;
- statements that a court "will" rule a particular way;
- claims that a person or agency is corrupt, dishonest, retaliatory, or unlawful unless the statement is clearly attributed to a source or framed as an allegation;
- rankings or quality judgments about attorneys or legal-help providers;
- fabricated citations, case names, quotations, or source links.

## Disclaimer standard

Educational and research content should make clear that it is informational and is not a substitute for legal advice. The disclaimer should not be used to excuse unsupported factual or legal claims.

## Release checklist

Before publishing or materially revising a guide:

- [ ] All substantive legal claims have an identified source.
- [ ] Washington/federal jurisdiction is clear.
- [ ] Time-sensitive law or procedure has been checked.
- [ ] No placeholder or fabricated source remains.
- [ ] Primary authority is preferred where available.
- [ ] Facts, allegations, inferences, disputes, and unknowns are not conflated.
- [ ] Links point to the intended source.
- [ ] User-facing instructions are actionable and understandable.
- [ ] Disclaimer is present where appropriate.
- [ ] Navigation points to the canonical guide or research destination.
- [ ] Production lint/build passes before release.
